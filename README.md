# fmtv-spotify-login

The phone-side sign-in page for Free My TV's Spotify login, served at https://chillmati.github.io/fmtv-spotify-login/ (also the redirect URI in the Spotify app's settings).

The TV shows a QR code that opens this page with its Spotify client ID, a one-time state and a PKCE code challenge. The page waits for the person to tap *Continue to Spotify* (it never goes there by itself), and Spotify always shows its approval screen (`show_dialog=true`): whoever made a link holds its verifier, so a sign-in must never finish without the person's say. When Spotify sends the phone back, the page checks the state and posts the sign-in code to the ntfy.sh topic `fmtv-<state>`, where the TV picks it up (or, after 15 s with no answer from ntfy, offers *Send again*). A sign-in that ends without a code (cancelled, or a Spotify error) posts `error <reason>` to the same topic instead, which the TV shows as cancelled; the page posts nothing for a state this tab didn't start.

It holds no secrets: no client secret (PKCE needs none), no keys, no tracking, no outside files. The code it passes on is single-use, short-lived and useless without the code verifier, which never leaves the TV.

To delete it: delete this repository (Settings > Danger Zone > Delete this repository), or turn off Pages (Settings > Pages > Source: None). Then remove the redirect URI from the Spotify app at developer.spotify.com/dashboard. Spotify sign-in on the TV stops working until another page takes its place.

## guest/: guests add music

`guest/` is the page a party guest opens from the TV's Guests QR code, served at https://chillmati.github.io/fmtv-spotify-login/guest/#t=<topic> (the topic is 20 characters of `a-z2-7` that the TV makes; without a valid one, the page only says to scan the code on the TV). The guest may type a name (up to 24 characters, remembered in this browser's localStorage for next time), pastes a Spotify link from Share > Copy link (an open.spotify.com link to a song, album, artist or playlist, with or without `intl-xx/` and `?si=...`, or a `spotify:<kind>:<id>` URI) and taps Send. Short `spotify.link` addresses (which some Spotify apps copy instead) can't be read without fetching them, so the page asks the guest to open the link in a new browser tab and paste the open.spotify.com address it lands on. The page posts `{"v":1,"uri":"spotify:<kind>:<id>","name":"<name or empty>"}` as text/plain to the ntfy.sh topic `fmtv-guest-<topic>`, which the TV listens on; the TV queues a song and plays a playlist, album or artist with its owner's Spotify Premium.

It holds no secrets either: no Spotify account, token or key is involved on the phone, and guests need no account. Anyone who has the QR code (or the link inside it) can send links to that TV's topic for as long as the TV listens on it, and read what others sent there: ntfy.sh topics are open to whoever knows the name, and the topic's 100 random bits are what keep everyone else out. The TV decides what to do with what arrives.

ntfy.sh limits requests by IP address (60, then one every 5 s), and guests on the party Wi-Fi share the TV's public address. So the TV must listen with one long-lived streaming GET on `https://ntfy.sh/fmtv-guest-<topic>/json` (or poll no faster than every 20 to 30 s), which leaves room for guests' posts. When ntfy.sh still answers 429, the page tells the guest to wait a minute and send again. ntfy.sh keeps messages for 12 hours, so the TV must ask with `since=<when it started listening>`, then `since=<last message id>`, or it replays old links.
